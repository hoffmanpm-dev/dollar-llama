# Voice Training Script for ElevenLabs

**Before you start**

Read this the way you want the clone to sound: the narrator of your videos, not a phone call. Calm, unhurried, a little warmth, like you're explaining something to one person across a desk. The clone copies whatever you give it, so if you rush or mumble, that's what you'll get back.

Quiet room, no fan or HVAC running, phone or mic about a foot from your mouth, slightly off to the side so your P's and B's don't pop. Don't whisper and don't project; use your normal speaking volume. If you stumble, pause a second and re-read the sentence from the start; you don't need to stop the recording. Leave the natural pauses in. Breaths are fine.

Instant cloning needs one to two minutes of clean audio, so Part 1 alone is enough for that. Professional cloning wants thirty minutes or more; for that, read all four parts, then keep going by reading two or three of your own blog posts aloud in the same voice. Your own writing is the best training material you have.

The whole thing below is about 1,100 words, roughly eight minutes at a relaxed pace.

---

## Part 1: The video narration (read this first, exactly as written)

Most people don't need a family office. But everyone could benefit from thinking like one.

I'm Paul. I've spent sixteen years inside family offices, advising some of the wealthiest families in the country.

The financial industry sells what it can package and scale. Mutual funds. Annuities. Insurance. Robo portfolios. Not what actually fits your life.

My clients get advice that starts with their situation, not a product shelf. Independent. No products to sell. No commissions.

That thinking is useful whether you have a hundred million dollars, or a hundred thousand. The frameworks work at every size.

On Dollar Llama, you'll find investment strategy without the sales pitch, real estate from the trenches, and tax and estate ideas in plain language.

Why a llama? They're resilient, sure-footed, and they don't follow the herd.

No products. No affiliate links. No sponsored posts. One email every other week. Dollar Llama dot com. Let's build something.

## Part 2: About the blog (your own voice, conversational)

I started Dollar Llama because of a problem nobody in my industry likes to talk about. The people I work with every day get the best financial advice money can buy. It's independent, it's built around their actual lives, and there's nobody in the room trying to sell them anything. They can afford that, and honestly, they need it. Their situations are complicated enough to justify the cost.

Most people don't have that option. If you can't hire a family office, you're mostly left with whatever the industry can package and sell at scale. Mutual funds, annuities, insurance products, a robo account with a nice app. Those things exist because they can be distributed to a million people at once, not because they're the right answer for you.

But the thinking behind good advice isn't expensive. The questions to ask, the way to size up what you're being sold, the habit of starting with your own situation instead of a product shelf. That works whether you have a hundred million dollars or a hundred thousand. That's what I'm trying to share here.

I'm not selling anything. I'm a finance guy, a real estate investor, and a dad who thinks everyone deserves a straight answer about money.

---

## Part 3: A story (narrative pacing, a few numbers and place names)

A few weeks ago I was on Interstate 55 between St. Louis and Chicago, and everything stopped. Both lanes, a long line of brake lights, the kind of jam where you know the next half hour is gone.

A few cars back, a sedan started working. He cut right into a gap that was barely a gap. Two cars later he cut back left. Then right again. Every time a lane moved, he was in it. Horns, hard braking, a lurch, another horn. He looked like he was fighting the traffic, and the traffic was winning.

When the jam finally broke, he was a handful of cars ahead of me. That's it. Fifteen minutes of lane changes, and he had gained about the length of a driveway.

I understand that guy. In high school I drove twenty miles of busy interstate every day for three years, and I drove it exactly like that. I don't remember ever getting anywhere earlier. I do remember that it felt like I was doing something.

The market does the same thing to people. It gets stuck, or it just gets boring, and somebody notices that the other lane looks faster. A fund a friend mentioned. A stock that ran forty percent last quarter. So they change lanes. Then the new thing stalls, and they change back. Every move costs something. Taxes, fees, the gap between where they sold and where they bought. Most people end up a few car lengths from where they would have been anyway.

## Part 4: Variety (numbers, questions, lists, emphasis)

Let me give you a few numbers. Five hundred dollars a month, invested at seven percent, is about six hundred and ten thousand dollars after thirty years, and about one point three million after forty. The S&P 500 peaked on October ninth, 2007, and didn't get back to that level until March of 2013. That's five and a half years. Home prices took ten years and two months to recover from their 2006 peak.

Here's the question I ask every client: are you trying to grow your money, or keep it? Those are different jobs. Capital efficiency is everything for growth and nearly irrelevant for preservation. Do you know which one you're playing?

Real estate is not for everyone. It takes capital, time, risk tolerance, and often a willingness to get your hands dirty. My wife runs the operations, and she's better at it than I am. I handle the analysis. If the midnight toilet call sounds like a nightmare, that's useful information. If it sounds like a few text messages to the right plumber, maybe it's for you.

Good investing is boring. Pick a sensible lane. Stay in it most of the time. Find something better to do with the trip than the brake pedal.

Thanks for listening. Welcome to Dollar Llama.
